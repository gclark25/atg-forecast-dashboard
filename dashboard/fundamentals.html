Δ = Metis median minus ERCOT's forecast. The signal compares that gap with Metis's own uncertainty (the p10 to p90 spread, as a standard deviation), averaged over the window: within 0.4 is in line, 0.4 to 1.0 leaning, over 1.0 strong. Tightness is the net-load gap. Outages are left out of it by default, because Metis's outage number sits above ERCOT's figure in nearly every hour, by an amount that is not constant, even though both use the same definition; that looks like a modelling difference rather than a tightness signal. The control above adds them. Available capacity and reserve margin already net those effects, so they are shown beside it as cross-checks instead of being added in. The wind, solar, outage and capacity choices start on the closest match the self-checks find.

Track record

Every hour the dashboard saves Metis's forecast and ERCOT's forecast as it stood when Metis was issued (the postings a forecaster issuing at :00 would have had), then scores both against what actually happened. This is the evidence for how much weight a gap deserves. It is collected in the background and nothing else on this page depends on it.

How to read this

  • Timing. Metis timestamps its hours by their start, and ERCOT's hour-ending rows are converted to the same start, so a Metis 4:00 PM point is compared with ERCOT's HE17. The alignment check below confirms this from the data.
  • Different vintages. The two sides are issued on different schedules (Metis hourly from :00, ERCOT's reports around :55, :00 and :30), so part of any gap is simply newer information on one side. The feed health card shows how old each side is.
  • Percentiles. In the hour-by-hour tooltips, "sits at Metis p23" means ERCOT's number falls at the 23rd percentile of Metis's own forecast distribution for that hour. That is the strength of the signal for the system-level rows, which carry all 21 quantile levels.
  • What Metis is. Per the vendor (Oct 29), Metis is primarily a price model. Its load, wind, solar, outage and capacity forecasts come out as by-products of the same output head, which does not know which target it is predicting or the hour of day. Treat a gap as a prompt to look, not a trade signal, until these forecasts have been scored against actuals.
  • What Metis uses. Outages: the sum of ERCOT's four zonal Total Resource MW columns (NP3-233-CD), without IRR or new-equipment outages. Capacity: the sum of the four zonal CapGenRes columns (NP3-763-CD), not availCapGen, with ERCOT's own value from the report issued an hour earlier as the label. The self-checks above test these.
  • Not covered. Metis's net imports have no ERCOT forecast to compare with, so they appear only in the curve chart.
